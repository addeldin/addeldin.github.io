## General

The **Pitt Games Reading Group** meets once or twice per semester and is open to students and faculty/staff at the University of Pittsburgh.

When we meet, we will discuss a game played in advance of the meeting, and then consider its design, story, mechanics, aesthetic, and more. We will also discuss a supplemental reading that gives useful context to the game under focus.

This group is a great opportunity for aspiring designers, avid game players, particularly unique game enjoyers, and those interested in the study of games to gather and explore the medium together, through examples that engage us in novel and unexpected ways.

## Next Meeting

### Fall 2026 (October)

**Meeting Details:** October 7th, 2026, 5:00 – 6:00PM, Lawrence Hall 232

**Game:** *Umurangi Generation* (2020) by Origame Digital

![Umurangi Generation banner art](/assets/img/umurangi-generation.jpg)

**Reading:** excerpts from *On Photography* by Susan Sontag

**Description:** For our next meeting, we will be playing *Umurangi Generation* (2020), “a first person photography game in the shitty future.” Follow your heart (or a list of bounties) as you navigate and capture a mix of changing cyberpunk locales with your camera—swapping lenses, zooming and focusing, changing color balance and exposure. You can unlock new features, abilities, lenses, and more as you earn depressingly little money for your artistic endeavors and see the narrative world develop around you. Welcome to the future, and may it be as bright as your flash!

**Discussion Questions:**

* What do you think of this game’s structure as a mode of storytelling? Do you see a narrative here? Did you feel engaged with the narrative as such? Would you say this game has ludo-narrative harmony?
* Through this game, do you see significance in the game’s setting and main character identity as New Zealand/Maori (which also match the developer’s identity)? Does that impact or have presence in the game? 
* Does this game make you think differently about photography? Its use in the world or its impact? Do you think this adequately gamifies photography?
